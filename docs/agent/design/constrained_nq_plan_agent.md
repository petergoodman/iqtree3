## 0. Answer first

The constrained estimate is one well-defined object once the likelihood protocol is fixed: the maximizer, over the 360-dimensional family $\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$ of positive, unit-mean-rate 20-state generators whose stationary distribution is the prescribed $\pi^*$, of the nQMaker objective (the unweighted sum of per-alignment rooted-tree log-likelihoods with $\pi^*$ as the root distribution), profiled over the nuisance parameters that nQMaker already optimizes. The chart used to parameterize $\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$ does not change this estimand (Theorem 6), so the chart is an engineering choice, and the only places nQMaker changes are inside step 3a of the paper's algorithm: the variable vector handed to BFGS, the map from that vector to $Q$, the treatment of `state_freq`, the parameter count, and the seed mapping. Steps 1, 2, 3b and 4 of nQMaker are untouched.

Recommended chart for the IQ-TREE implementation: the row-shape chart C2 (section 3.1), which is `NONREV` with one pinned rate per row instead of one pinned rate overall, and with the 20 row scales computed from the constraint by one linear solve that replaces the existing `computeStateFreqFromQMatrix` call. It is total (every positive coordinate vector gives a valid member of $\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$), bijective (every member is reached exactly once), needs no inner iteration and no gauge bookkeeping beyond the per-row pin, normalizes automatically, keeps every optimizer coordinate strictly positive so that IQ-TREE's relative finite-difference step and its rate box apply unchanged, and its seed is the row-normalization of any matrix whatsoever. The balancing chart C1 (Doc A's C1, Doc B's T3; section 3.2) is mathematically equally complete, and its advantage is that the reversible submodel is the coordinate subspace $h\equiv 0$; its costs inside IQ-TREE are a 19-dimensional Newton solve per likelihood evaluation, signed coordinates that IQ-TREE's finite-difference rule handles badly near zero (section 3.2.5), a gauge whose natural bounds are not the raw-rate bounds (section 3.2.4), and the fact that the linked-model optimizer path computes its gradient in `Optimization::derivativeFunk` on the `PartitionModel` object, so a model-class override of the derivative rule would not take effect in nQMaker (section 4.6). C1, a linear balanced-flux formulation, and constrained EM should exist as external Python reference implementations that the IQ-TREE class is validated against; none of them needs to be compiled into IQ-TREE for Project 2.

Three findings that change how the earlier documents should be read. First, the nQMaker candidate set of step 1 does not need to be constrained: those matrices only select a per-alignment model and build trees in step 2, and the seed of step 3a is mapped into $\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$ by row-normalization in one line (section 5). Second, Doc A's claim that $h_{\max}=12$ contains the `NONREV` box is false, as the audit said; the gauge-fixed tilt of a matrix with all raw rates in $[10^{-4},100]$ reaches $20.72$ (check 4), while the tilts of the shipped `NQ.pfam` never exceed $0.87$ (check `check_builtin_matrices`), so the issue is real but not pressing. Third, the IQ-TREE linked-model constructor already applies a reversible-style frequency swap to the seed (`adaptStateFrequency`, section 4.3), which is valid for the reversible LG seed and invalid for a nonreversible seed (check 7); the constrained class must bypass it, which it does by declaring `FREQ_USER_DEFINED`.

The scientific caveat that survives every audit: imposing $\pi^*$ pins the marginal composition at every node to $\pi^*$ (Theorem 5), so when the cleaned training data have a different composition the constrained model is misspecified in its first moment, and the 171 circulation coordinates can absorb part of that misfit; a purely reversible generating process fitted at the wrong composition acquires nonzero circulation (check 11). Any nonreversibility reported by the constrained fit is confounded with the constraint until a nonreversible simulation at the real composition shift has bounded the artifact (section 8).

## 1. Objects, likelihood, and the estimand

### 1.1 Objects

$n=20$ states in IQ-TREE order `A R N D C Q E G H I L K M F P S T W Y V` (`alignment/alignment.cpp`, `symbols_protein`) [source]. $\Delta^\circ$ is the open simplex. A generator $Q$ has $q_{ij}>0$ for $i\ne j$ and $q_{ii}=-\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}$. Its unique stationary distribution is $\pi(Q)$, defined by $\pi Q=0$ (row vector convention). The mean rate at $\pi$ is $m(Q,\pi)=-\sum_i\pi_iq_{ii}$. The flux is $F_{ij}=\pi_iq_{ij}$ for $i\ne j$. The exit rate of state $i$ is $\lambda_i=-q_{ii}$ and the embedded jump chain is $p_{ij}=q_{ij}/\lambda_i$.

$$\mathcal G=\{Q:\ q_{ij}>0,\ Q\mathbf 1=0,\ m(Q,\pi(Q))=1\},\qquad \mathcal G_{\pi^*}=\{Q\in\mathcal G:\ \pi^*Q=0\}.$$

IQ-TREE's `NONREV` stores the 380 off-diagonal entries row-major in `rates[]`, skipping the diagonal (`model/modelmarkov.cpp:1256-1264`), derives the diagonal from row sums, re-solves $\pi$ from $Q$ on every likelihood evaluation, and rescales to $m=1$ (`decomposeRateMatrixNonrev`, lines 1246-1282) [source].

### 1.2 The nQMaker objective as implemented

The paper's algorithm [paper, Fig. 1 and the numbered list on pp. 1112-1113]: (1) candidate set $\mathbf Q=\{LG, JTT, WAG\}$, $Q^{BEST}:=LG$; (2) for each alignment $D_i$ choose $Q_i\in\mathbf Q$ and site-rate model $V_i$ by model selection and infer an ML tree $T_i$ (rooted if $Q_i$ is nonreversible; for clade data sets one edge-linked topology); (3) with $V_i,T_i$ fixed, alternate (a) estimate $Q^{NEW}$ with branch lengths $L_i$ fixed and (b) estimate $L_i$ with $Q^{NEW}$ fixed, until the log-likelihood gain is below 0.1; (4) $Q^{BEST}:=Q^{NEW}$; if the Pearson correlation between successive matrices is below 0.999, add $Q^{BEST}$ to $\mathbf Q$ and repeat from (2), else return. The paper's commands are `iqtree2 -S ALN_DIR -mset LG,WAG,JTT -cmax 4` (steps 1 and 2) and `iqtree2 -S ALN_DIR.best_model.nex -te ALN_DIR.treefile --model-joint NONREV+FO` (step 3), with `-p` in place of `-S` for the clade data sets [paper, p. 1113-1114].

In the source, step 3 is one run. `-te` sets `min_iterations = 0` and `stop_condition = SC_FIXED_ITERATION` (`utils/tools.cpp:4699-4706`), so no topology search runs. `-S` makes `partition_type = TOPO_UNLINKED` and the model factory is `PartitionModel` (`tree/iqtree.cpp:1065-1066`); `-p` gives `PartitionModelPlen` (line 1068). `--model-joint NAME` sets `params.model_joint` and `link_model = true` (`utils/tools.cpp:5023-5029`). Each partition's `ModelFactory` constructor replaces its own model string by `model_joint` and extracts the `+F...` part of `model_joint` as that partition's frequency specification (`model/modelfactory.cpp:284-305`), so a literal `+F{p1,...,p20}` in `--model-joint` reaches every partition [source; this resolves an item the earlier documents left unverified]. `+F{...}` sets `FREQ_USER_DEFINED` with the vector as `freq_params` (`modelfactory.cpp:500-509`), which `ModelProtein::init` reads through `readStateFreq`, rejecting entries outside $[0,1]$ and renormalizing to sum one with a warning (`modelprotein.cpp:1240-1244`, `modelmarkov.cpp:1761-1796`) [source].

The joint objective is computed by `PartitionModel::targetFunk` (`model/partitionmodel.cpp:299-338`): every partition whose model has the linked name evaluates `part_model->targetFunk(x)` on the same coordinate vector inside an OpenMP loop, and the per-partition values are summed serially in fixed order [source]. The per-partition `ModelMarkov::targetFunk` (`modelmarkov.cpp:1091-1118`) writes the coordinates into the model, re-decomposes, invalidates partial likelihoods, returns $10^{30}$ if any `state_freq` entry lies in $(0,$ `min_state_freq`$=10^{-4})$ or is negative, and otherwise returns minus the tree log-likelihood [source]. The rooted nonreversible kernel fills the root leaf's partial vector with `model->getStateFrequency` and applies no transition matrix to it (`tree/phylokernelnonrev.h:676-682` and the analogous blocks) [source], so `state_freq` is the root distribution. With $\pi^*$ held in `state_freq`, the per-partition log-likelihood is

$$\ell_k(Q,\psi_k)=\sum_{p}n_{kp}\log\Big(\sum_{c}\rho_{kc}\sum_a\pi^*_a\,\ell^{(k,p,c)}_{\mathrm{root}}(a)\Big),$$

with pattern multiplicities $n_{kp}$, rate categories $c$ of weight $\rho_{kc}$ and rate $r_{kc}$ scaling branch lengths, and the pruning recursion in the state basis with explicit $e^{r_{kc}t\,Q}$ matrices (transposed for the child pointing toward the root) [source]. The nQMaker objective is $\ell(Q,\psi)=\sum_k\ell_k(Q,\psi_k)$.

### 1.3 The estimand

Fix the protocol $\mathcal P$: the training alignments, their rooted topologies and root edges, the branch-length linkage (`-S`: separate trees; `-p`: shared topology with partition rate multipliers), the site-rate models, the root distribution ($\pi^*$), the box bounds on the optimizer coordinates, and the nuisance domain $\Psi$. Then

$$\widehat Q_{\pi^*}=\arg\max_{Q\in\mathcal G_{\pi^*}}\ \ell^*(Q),\qquad \ell^*(Q)=\sup_{\psi\in\Psi}\ell(Q,\psi).$$

Everything about how this is computed (chart, optimizer, alternation) is the subject of sections 3 to 6; nothing there changes the estimand except the box bounds, which are part of $\mathcal P$ (Theorem 6, remark 3). The argmax may be a set; the objective is not concave (Theorem 8); the maximum over the closure may lie at a zero rate (Theorem 7). Existence of an interior maximizer is not guaranteed.

## 2. Theorems about the family and the estimand

All proofs are from the definitions in 1.1. Where a check name appears, `verify_constrained_nq.py` confirms the statement numerically at $n=20$ and the value is reported in section 12.

### Theorem 1 (dimension and convexity of the fiber) [proved; checked 1.*]

Let $q\in\mathbb R^{380}$ be the off-diagonal rates. Stationarity at $\pi$ is the linear system $C_\pi q=0$ with $(C_\pi q)_j=\sum_{i\ne j}\pi_iq_{ij}-\pi_j\sum_{k\ne j}q_{jk}$. Since $\sum_j(C_\pi q)_j=\sum_{i\ne j}\pi_iq_{ij}-\sum_j\pi_j\sum_{k\ne j}q_{jk}=0$ identically, the rank is at most $19$; it equals $19$ because the image is exactly $\mathbf 1^\perp$ (take $q$ with a single nonzero entry $q_{ij}$: the drift is $\pi_i(e_j-e_i)$, and these span $\mathbf 1^\perp$ for a connected support). The mean-rate functional $m_\pi(q)=\sum_{i\ne j}\pi_iq_{ij}$ is not a combination of the rows of $C_\pi$, because it is positive on the feasible point $q_{ij}=\pi_j$ where every row of $C_\pi$ vanishes. Hence $\dim\mathcal G_\pi=380-19-1=360$, and $\mathcal G_\pi$ is the strictly positive part of an affine subspace, therefore relatively open and convex. The unnormalized cone has dimension 361. The reversible members ($\pi_iq_{ij}=\pi_jq_{ji}$) form the positive part of a $190-1=189$ dimensional affine subspace, leaving $171$ nonreversible dimensions. Correction carried over from the audit: the image of the drift map is $\mathbf 1^\perp$, not $\pi^\perp$ (check 1.drift_dot_pi_nonzero).

### Theorem 2 (the fiber is the same polytope for every $\pi$) [proved; checked 12.*]

The flux map $q\mapsto F$, $F_{ij}=\pi_iq_{ij}$, is a linear bijection of $\mathcal G_\pi$ onto $\mathcal C=\{F>0\ \text{off-diagonal}:\ \text{in-flow}=\text{out-flow at every state},\ \sum F=1\}$, which does not depend on $\pi$. Hence all fibers are affinely isomorphic, and holding $F$ fixed while changing $\pi$ (the T1 transport) maps $\mathcal G_{\pi_a}$ onto $\mathcal G_{\pi_b}$ exactly. The closure $\overline{\mathcal C}$ is a compact polytope; its vertices are the uniform flows on directed simple cycles [recalled, standard flow decomposition]. Every flux entry obeys $F_{ij}\le\sum_{k\ne i}F_{ik}=\sum_{k\ne i}F_{ki}\le 1-\sum_{k\ne i}F_{ik}$, so $F_{ij}\le\tfrac12$ and $q_{ij}\le 1/(2\pi_i)$, with equality only on a two-state cycle boundary (check 4.max_flux_entry_over_samples: $0.40$ over 200 random members at $n=6$ [instance]). Doc A's looser statement that $q_{ij}$ can reach $1/\pi_i$ is incorrect, as the audit noted.

### Theorem 3 (the fixed asymmetric frequency swap fails) [proved; checked 7.*]

For symmetric $R$, $q_{ij}=R_{ij}\pi_j$ satisfies $\pi_iq_{ij}=\pi_jq_{ji}$ at every $\pi$, so the reversible frequency swap is exact. For a fixed matrix $A$ with $q_{ij}=A_{ij}\pi_j$, the drift is $(\pi Q)_j=\pi_j[(A^\top-A)\pi]_j$, which vanishes for all $\pi$ in an open set only if $A=A^\top$. IQ-TREE's `ModelMarkov::adaptStateFrequency` (`modelmarkov.cpp:914-930`) applies exactly this swap, $q_{ij}\leftarrow q_{ij}\,\pi^{\rm new}_j/\pi^{\rm old}_j$, to a nonreversible model's `rates[]`; it is stationary at $\pi^{\rm new}$ for the LG-derived seed and not for a nonreversible seed (check 7: residual $3.5\times10^{-17}$ versus $3.09$ [instance]). Under `NONREV+FO` this is harmless because $\pi$ is re-solved from $Q$ afterwards; it matters for the constrained class (section 4.3).

### Theorem 4 (scale is a gauge) [proved]

For $c>0$, $e^{(t/c)(cQ)}=e^{tQ}$, and the root term does not involve the scale. With all branch lengths free (and, under `-p`, the partition multipliers free), maximizing over the unnormalized cone and normalizing afterwards gives the same set of models as maximizing over $\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$, provided the rescaled branch lengths remain inside their bounds. IQ-TREE's normalization in `decomposeRateMatrixNonrev` selects the representative $m=1$ on every evaluation [source].

### Theorem 5 (stationary marginals) [proved; checked 8]

For $Q\in\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$ with root distribution $\pi^*$, $\pi^*e^{tQ}=\pi^*$ for every $t$, so the marginal distribution at every node, including every tip, is $\pi^*$, for every rooted tree, branch lengths, and rate mixture sharing the same $Q$. If the population composition $p_a$ of the retained data at some tip differs from $\pi^*$, every model in the family is misspecified and $\mathrm{KL}(P_0\|P_Q)\ge\mathrm{KL}(p_a\|\pi^*)>0$ (chain rule for KL through the tip marginal). An empirical difference in a finite alignment is not by itself evidence of a population difference; the population statement is what the argument needs.

### Theorem 6 (chart invariance of the estimand) [proved]

Let $B:\Theta\to\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$ be onto and $L$ any function on $\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$. Then $\sup_\Theta L\circ B=\sup_{\mathcal G_{\pi^*}}L$ and $\arg\max L\circ B=B^{-1}(\arg\max L)$; if $B$ is injective the argmax sets are in bijection. If $B$ is a $C^2$ diffeomorphism onto the relatively open set $\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$, then critical points and their types correspond (Hessians are congruent at critical points by the chain rule). Remarks. (1) Not invariant: optimizer iterates, basins, conditioning, stopping tolerances expressed in coordinates. (2) A box $[l,u]$ on one chart's coordinates is not a box in another chart; bounded feasible sets differ and so do the bounded estimands when a bound is active; the same holds for coordinate penalties. (3) Disagreement between two complete charts on the same data is evidence about optimization or bounds, not about the model.

### Theorem 7 (existence on the closure) [proved]

For fixed finite trees and nuisance parameters in a compact $\Psi$, pattern probabilities are continuous on $\overline{\mathcal G_{\pi^*}}\times\Psi$, so the likelihood attains its maximum on the compact closure $\overline{\mathcal C}$ (Theorem 2). The maximizer may have zero rates and need not be irreducible. Interior charts approach such a point asymptotically; IQ-TREE's box replaces it by a floor. Compactness of the actual nuisance domain has to be checked, not assumed from the word "bounds".

### Theorem 8 (the constrained likelihood is not concave) [proved; checked 5.*]

Three states, uniform $\pi$, $Q(x)$ the circulant with $q_{12}=q_{23}=q_{31}=(1+x)/2$ and $q_{21}=q_{32}=q_{13}=(1-x)/2$, $|x|<1$: every member is in $\mathcal G_{1/3}$ (mean rate 1, stationary at uniform, check 5.circulant_stationary_uniform) and $x\mapsto Q(x)$ is an affine line inside the fiber. On a rooted two-tip tree with branch lengths $t_1=0.1$, $t_2=5.1$ and the observed pattern $(0,0)$, $L_{00}(x)=\tfrac19[1+2e^{-3(t_1+t_2)/2}\cos(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}(t_2-t_1)x)]$ (check 5.circulant_formula_error $2.2\times10^{-16}$), and at $x_0=2\pi/(5\sqrt3)$ the second derivative of $\log L_{00}$ is $Ak^2/(1-A)=0.01538>0$ with $A=2e^{-3(t_1+t_2)/2}$, $k=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}(t_2-t_1)$ (check 5.second_derivative). Positive curvature along an affine line in the fiber rules out concavity. Embedding this block in a 20-state generator with small symmetric cross rates preserves positive curvature by continuity. Consequence: no optimizer has a global guarantee; report multiple starts (D9).

### Theorem 9 (nested tests inside the fiber) [proved]

(i) The reversible submodel of $\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$ is IQ-TREE's `GTR20+F{`$\pi^*$`}` after normalization; the test has $360-189=171$ parameters in $Q$. Under a reversible null the likelihood is invariant to the root position (pulley principle) and, with the root edge fixed, depends on the two root-adjacent branch lengths $t_u,t_v$ only through $t_u+t_v$; under the alternative the joint law of the root children is $\Pi e^{t_uQ^\leftarrow}e^{t_vQ}$ with $Q^\leftarrow=\Pi^{-1}Q^\top\Pi$ the time reversal, and its derivative in the split at fixed sum is $\Pi e^{t_uQ^\leftarrow}(Q^\leftarrow-Q)e^{t_vQ}$, zero for all $t_u,t_v$ iff $Q^\leftarrow=Q$. The split is therefore a parameter present only under the alternative; the $\chi^2_{171}$ reference is not justified unless root edge and split are fixed identically under both hypotheses, and even then it requires an interior, identifiable true parameter with nonsingular information. Otherwise bootstrap the actual protocol. IQ-TREE's branch-parameter count subtracts one extra degree of freedom for a rooted reversible tree for this reason (`tree/phylotree.cpp:2608-2634`) [source]. The audit's warning is retained: a nonzero derivative proves local sensitivity, not global or joint identifiability (a circulant alias exists). (ii) $\mathcal G_{\pi^*}\subset\mathcal G$ is a 19-constraint nested restriction of `NONREV` as unbounded models with the same root convention; $2(\ell_{\rm NONREV}-\ell_{\pi^*})$ measures the composition cost of the constraint on the training data. With bounds active the bounded models are not nested, and representability of each optimum in the other's box must be checked directly.

## 3. Charts of $\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$

A chart is a map from an optimizer coordinate space onto $\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$. By Theorem 6 all complete charts target the same estimand; the differences below are the whole basis for the choice.

### 3.1 C2: the row-shape (jump-chain) chart, recommended for IQ-TREE

#### 3.1.1 Definition

Let $\mathcal P$ be the set of $n\times n$ matrices $P$ with zero diagonal, positive off-diagonal entries, and unit row sums. For $P\in\mathcal P$ let $\nu=\nu(P)$ be its stationary distribution, $\nu P=\nu$, $\nu\mathbf 1=1$. Define

$$\lambda_i=\frac{\nu_i}{\pi^*_i},\qquad q_{ij}=\lambda_ip_{ij}\ (i\ne j),\qquad q_{ii}=-\lambda_i.$$

Equivalently $F_{ij}=\nu_ip_{ij}$ and $q_{ij}=F_{ij}/\pi^*_i$.

In `NONREV` terms: the 380 stored rates are read up to a common factor per row (the row shape $p_{i\cdot}$), and the 20 row factors (the exit rates $\lambda_i$) are the ones determined by the constraint. The free coordinates are the 380 rates with one entry per row pinned, that is $380-20=360$ of them.

#### 3.1.2 Theorem 10 (C2 is total, exact, bijective, normalized, smooth) [proved; checked 2.*]

For every $P\in\mathcal P$: $P$ is irreducible, so $\nu$ exists, is unique and positive (Perron and Frobenius); in-flow at $j$ is $\sum_i\nu_ip_{ij}=\nu_j$ and out-flow is $\nu_j\sum_kp_{jk}=\nu_j$, so $F=\mathrm{diag}(\nu)P$ is balanced with $\sum F=1$; hence $\pi^*Q=0$, all off-diagonals are positive, and $m(Q,\pi^*)=\sum_i\pi^*_i\lambda_i=\sum_i\nu_i=1$ without a normalization step (checks 2.c2_stationarity_residual $2\times10^{-16}$, 2.c2_mean_rate_minus_1 $6\times10^{-16}$). Conversely, for $Q\in\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$ put $p_{ij}=q_{ij}/(-q_{ii})$ and $\mu_i=\pi^*_i(-q_{ii})$; then $(\mu P)_j=\sum_{i\ne j}\pi^*_iq_{ij}=\pi^*_j(-q_{jj})=\mu_j$ by stationarity and $\sum\mu=m(Q,\pi^*)=1$, so $\mu=\nu(P)$ and the build returns $Q$ (check 2.c2_roundtrip_P, 2.c2_nu_equals_pi_times_exit). Injectivity: the jump chain of the built $Q$ is $P$. Row scale is a gauge: multiplying any row of the stored rates by a positive constant leaves $P$, hence $Q$, unchanged (check 2.c2_row_scale_is_gauge). The finite-difference Jacobian of the map from 360 ratio coordinates to $Q$ has rank 360 (check 2.c2_jacobian_rank). $\nu$ is a rational function of $P$ with nonvanishing denominator on $\mathcal P$; with $Z=(I-P+\mathbf 1\nu)^{-1}$, which exists on $\mathcal P$ (a left null vector $x$ has $x\mathbf 1=0$ and $x(I-P)=0$, so $x\propto\nu$, so $x=0$), the derivative for a row-sum-preserving perturbation is $d\nu=\nu\,dP\,Z$ (check 2.c2_dnu_formula_error $1.1\times10^{-10}$) [recalled for the classical form: Schweitzer 1968, doi:10.2307/3212261]. Hence the chart is $C^\infty$ and the analytic gradient, if ever wanted, is $dq_{ij}=d\lambda_i\,p_{ij}+\lambda_i\,dp_{ij}$ with $d\lambda=d\nu/\pi^*$.

#### 3.1.3 Coordinates for IQ-TREE

Store the 380 rates as `NONREV` does. Choose for each row $i$ a reference column $\mathrm{ref}(i)$; the free coordinates are $x_{ij}=q^{\rm raw}_{ij}$ for $j\ne i,\mathrm{ref}(i)$, with $q^{\rm raw}_{i,\mathrm{ref}(i)}$ pinned. The build reads $p_{ij}=q^{\rm raw}_{ij}/\sum_{k\ne i}q^{\rm raw}_{ik}$. Recommended reference: the column holding the seed row's largest entry, fixed at construction, saved in the checkpoint, and pinned at the value `AA_SCALE = 10.0` used by `rescaleRates` (`modelprotein.cpp:1090-1105`) [source]; the other entries of the row then start in $(0,10]$ and the existing box $[10^{-4},100]$ means each rate may range from $10^{-5}$ to $10$ times the row's seed maximum, which mirrors `NONREV`'s effective relative range (its seed is rescaled to maximum 10 by `rescaleRates`, and the optimizer's lower bound $10^{-4}$ then applies to every trial point through `fixBound`). Built-in matrices fit comfortably: the largest within-row dynamic range is $6.1\times10^3$ for LG at LG's frequencies and $4.6\times10^3$ for `NQ.pfam` [check_builtin_matrices, instance]. A fixed reference column that is a rare substitution direction would instead push other entries toward the upper bound, so the argmax rule, or an explicit re-gauging of each row at construction, is required.

All coordinates are strictly positive, so `Optimization::derivativeFunk`'s relative step $10^{-4}|x_i|$ behaves exactly as it does for `NONREV` today (check 6.fd_rule_on_positive_ratio_coordinate: relative error $\le1.6\times10^{-4}$ at $x=1,10^{-2},10^{-4}$ [instance]), and `fixBound` clamping and `bound_check = false` carry over unchanged.

#### 3.1.4 Bounds, boundary and hazards

The box on ratio coordinates is a different set from `NONREV`'s box on raw rates (Theorem 6, remark 2); both are numerical safeguards, not the model. A fit at a bound is a reportable outcome (D7). Zero-rate maximizers (Theorem 7) appear as coordinates at the lower bound. Near-reducible chains (a row shape concentrated on few entries in a way that nearly disconnects the state graph) make $\nu$ ill-conditioned, but the corresponding $Q$ is itself nearly reducible, and `NONREV` has the same behavior; the $10^{-4}$ floor keeps the chain irreducible. The reversible submodel is the nonlinear submanifold $\nu_ip_{ij}=\nu_jp_{ji}$, not a coordinate subspace; the reversible fit is obtained from IQ-TREE's existing `GTR20+F{`$\pi^*$`}` and read into the chart as a seed (3.1.5), so nothing is lost for the nested comparison. Holding $P$ fixed across fibers is the flux-preserving transport T1 (Theorem 2), which is relevant only to the comparators of section 9.

#### 3.1.5 Seeding

Any matrix seeds the chart by row-normalization, whatever its own equilibrium: $p_{ij}=q^{\rm seed}_{ij}/\sum_{k\ne i}q^{\rm seed}_{ik}$ (check 7.c2_seed_from_foreign_Q_stationary $1.6\times10^{-16}$). The resulting start is a valid member of $\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$, but which member depends on the seed matrix, including its composition, because the column factor $\pi_j$ in a reversible seed is not a row scale. Two reversible seeds must be distinguished. Seed A: build $q_{ij}=R_{ij}\pi^*_j$ (exchangeabilities of LG, WAG, JTT or a fitted `GTR20`, combined with the target); this matrix is already in the fiber, the chart returns it unchanged, and it equals the $h\equiv0$ slice of C1 and the structure of `GTR20+F{`$\pi^*$`}`. Seed B: row-normalize LG at LG's own frequencies (what `setReversible(false)` produces when `state_freq` still holds LG's vector); this is the flux-preserving transport of LG to $\pi^*$, which is reversible at $\pi^*$ (symmetric flux stays symmetric) but with exchangeabilities $R_{ij}\pi^{LG}_i\pi^{LG}_j/(\pi^*_i\pi^*_j)$, not LG's. Both are legitimate starts; seed A is the recommended round-1 seed because it is the natural reversible null point, and it is obtained in IQ-TREE by setting `state_freq` to $\pi^*$ before calling `setReversible(false)` (which multiplies by the current `state_freq`, `modelmarkov.cpp:116-117`) [source]. The independent synthesis (`project2_iqtree3_technical_plan.md`, section 7.2) makes the same point. Nonreversible seeds (the previous round's $Q^{BEST}$, `NQ.pfam`) are row-normalized directly, which is the T1 transport of section 9; retargeting `NQ.pfam` to LG's composition this way changes entries by up to 20% [check_builtin_matrices, instance], a statement about seeds, not about the estimate.

### 3.2 C1: the balancing chart (Doc A's C1, Doc B's T3)

#### 3.2.1 Definition

Symmetric strengths $r_{ij}>0$ and antisymmetric tilts $h_{ij}=-h_{ji}$ give the pre-structure $M_{ij}=r_{ij}e^{h_{ij}}$ and $N_{ij}=\pi^*_iM_{ij}\pi^*_j$. Minimize the strictly convex $\Phi(g)=\sum_{i\ne j}N_{ij}e^{g_j-g_i}$ over $g\in\mathbb R^n$ with $g_n=0$; at the minimizer $g^*$ set $q_{ij}=M_{ij}\pi^*_je^{g^*_j-g^*_i}$ and normalize to $m=1$.

#### 3.2.2 Theorem 11 (existence, uniqueness, exactness, smoothness) [proved; checked 3.*]

$\Phi$ is a positive combination of exponentials of linear forms, hence smooth and convex, and invariant under $g\mapsto g+c\mathbf 1$. Its gradient at state $k$ is (column sum minus row sum) of $F=\mathrm{diag}(e^{-g})N\,\mathrm{diag}(e^{g})$, and its Hessian is the Laplacian of the connected graph with weights $F_{kl}+F_{lk}>0$, positive semidefinite with kernel $\mathbb R\mathbf 1$, so $\Phi$ is strictly convex on the slice. Coercivity on the slice: if $\|g\|\to\infty$ with $g_n=0$ then $\max g-\min g\to\infty$ and the term on the edge from the minimizer to the maximizer diverges. So $g^*$ exists and is unique, $\nabla\Phi(g^*)=0$ means $F$ is balanced, $\pi^*_iq_{ij}=F_{ij}$ gives $\pi^*Q=0$ exactly, and the implicit function theorem with the nonsingular reduced Laplacian gives $C^\infty$ dependence of $g^*$ on $(r,h)$. Damped Newton with a round-off allowance in the decrease test converged in 5 steps from a cold start at $n=20$ (check 3.c1_newton_iterations_cold [instance]; Doc A reports at most 6). The line-sum-symmetric scaling theorem of Eaves, Hoffman, Rothblum and Schneider (1985, doi:10.1007/BFb0121080) is the classical statement [recalled].

#### 3.2.3 Theorem 12 (gauge and the 360-dimensional chart) [proved; checked 3.*]

Two pre-structures build the same $Q$ iff $M'=\kappa D_cMD_c^{-1}$ for a scalar $\kappa>0$ and a positive diagonal $D_c$, that is $(r,h)\sim(\kappa r,\ h+\nabla\log c)$ with $(\nabla\log c)_{ij}=\log c_i-\log c_j$ (check 3.c1_gauge_orbit_invariance $6.7\times10^{-16}$). The slice $\{r_{n-1,n}=1,\ h_{1j}=0\ \forall j\}$ meets every orbit once, giving a bijection $\mathbb R^{189}\times\mathbb R^{171}\to\mathcal G_{\pi^*}$ with explicit inverse $M_{ij}=q_{ij}/\pi^*_j$, $r=\sqrt{MM^\top}$ entrywise, $h=\tfrac12\log(M/M^\top)$, then gauge-fix (check 3.c1_gauge_fixed_roundtrip $4.4\times10^{-16}$, 3.c1_jacobian_rank 360). $Q$ is reversible iff $F$ is symmetric iff $h$ is a gradient iff, in the gauge-fixed slice, $h\equiv0$; then $g^*=0$ and $Q=\mathcal N_{\pi^*}(r\,\mathrm{diag}\,\pi^*)$, IQ-TREE's reversible construction (checks 3.c1_h0_equals_R_diag_pi $0.0$, 3.c1_h0_gstar_is_zero). C1 and C2 agree on common fiber points to $4.5\times10^{-14}$ (check 3.c1_c2_agree_on_fiber_point). Derivative through the solve: for a perturbation $v=\delta\log r+\delta h$ on edges, with reduced incidence $D$ and unnormalized balanced flux $x$, $H\,\delta g=-D\,\mathrm{diag}(x)v$, $\delta x=\mathrm{diag}(x)(v+D^\top\delta g)$, $\delta f=(\delta x-f\,\mathbf 1^\top\delta x)/\mathbf 1^\top x$ (Doc B's formulas, re-derived by the implicit function theorem; Doc A's mixed-derivative shorthand should read $D_Mg^*=-[D_g\nabla_g\Phi]^{-1}D_M\nabla_g\Phi$).

#### 3.2.4 Bounds: the audit's correction, confirmed [checked 4.*]

Doc A bounded the raw pairwise tilt $h_{ij}=\tfrac12\log(\pi_iq_{ij}/(\pi_jq_{ji}))$ by $11.5$ for rates in $[10^{-4},100]$ and compositions above $10^{-4}$ and proposed $h_{\max}=12$. The optimizer coordinate is the gauge-fixed tilt $h'_{ij}=h_{ij}+h_{1i}-h_{1j}=\tfrac12\log\frac{q_{1i}q_{ij}q_{j1}}{q_{i1}q_{ji}q_{1j}}$, half a triangle affinity, in which the compositions cancel and three rate ratios multiply. The 20-state matrix with all off-diagonals 1 except $q_{12}=q_{23}=q_{31}=100$ and $q_{21}=q_{32}=q_{13}=10^{-4}$ is stationary at uniform, has every raw rate inside the box, raw $\max|h|=6.91$, and gauge-fixed $h'_{23}=\tfrac32\log10^6=20.72$ (check 4). The containment claim is false. For calibration, `NQ.pfam` at its own equilibrium has raw $\max|h|=0.94$ and gauge-fixed $\max|h'|=0.87$ [check_builtin_matrices, instance], so a bound of 12 would not have bitten on the published matrices, but no bound on $h'$ can be derived from the raw-rate box; it must be declared and its hits reported. Doc A's factor $1/\sqrt{\pi_i\pi_j}$ at $\pi_i=\pi_j=10^{-4}$ is $10^4$, not $10^2$.

#### 3.2.5 IQ-TREE's finite-difference rule on signed coordinates [source; checked 6.*]

`Optimization::derivativeFunk` (`utils/optimization.cpp:916-939`) uses $h_i=10^{-4}|x_i|$, or $10^{-4}$ when $x_i$ is exactly zero, one-sided forward. A signed coordinate approaching zero gets a step that collapses to round-off. On a 3-state two-tip log-likelihood of size $2\times10^4$ parameterized in C1 coordinates, the error of IQ-TREE's rule on $\partial\ell/\partial h_{23}$ (central-difference reference $-12.87$ at $h=10^{-2}$ and $-13.75$ at the three smaller tilts) is $4.1\times10^{-5}$ at $h=10^{-2}$, $4.7\times10^{-4}$ at $10^{-4}$, $0.070$ at $10^{-6}$ and $6.5$ at $10^{-8}$, the last a 47% error (check 6 [instance]). Doc A's remedy, storing $h+12$, gives an error of $0.053$ at every one of these points, larger than the unshifted error whenever $|h|\ge10^{-4}$, because the step $1.2\times10^{-3}$ inflates truncation error; the audit's objection stands. The clean remedies are positive coordinates (C2) or a derivative rule with an absolute floor, $h_i=\eta\max(s_i,|x_i|)$. A model-class override of `derivativeFunk` is possible (it is `virtual` in `Optimization`, `utils/optimization.h:145`) but is not reached in linked training, see 4.6. `ModelLieMarkov` already uses signed coordinates in $[-0.98,0.98]$ with this rule (`model/modelliemarkov.cpp:319-320, 886-896`) [source], so the hazard is not hypothetical inside IQ-TREE.

#### 3.2.6 Role

C1's advantages, the coordinate-subspace reversible submodel and the T3 transport comparator, are analysis conveniences that Project 2 obtains from `GTR20+F{`$\pi^*$`}` and from a Python decomposition of the fitted $\widehat Q$. Its costs inside IQ-TREE are the inner solve, the gauge, the bound question of 3.2.4 and the derivative rule of 3.2.5. Recommendation: implement C1 in the external Python oracle, where it validates the C2 class (two complete charts converging to the same $\widehat Q$ on the same data test the chart code and the optimizer, Theorem 6 remark 3). Adding a C1 class to IQ-TREE later is possible and costs nothing in the estimand.

### 3.3 Other formulations and their roles

Linear balanced-flux coordinates $f=f_0+Zz$ with $Z$ an orthonormal basis of $\ker C$ ($C$ the incidence rows plus the normalization row; $Z$ does not depend on $\pi^*$ because the incidence matrix does not) give the most transparent statement of the model; the inequalities $f>0$ couple the coordinates and require a feasible line search ($\alpha_{\max}=\min_{d_e<0}(f_e-\varepsilon_e)/(-d_e)$), which IQ-TREE's box clamping cannot provide. Role: external reference optimizer (SQP or reduced-space) on small data. Hamiltonian-cycle flux elimination (Doc A's C3) is the same thing with a particular non-orthonormal basis and a proper open domain; not recommended for IQ-TREE because a forward probe can leave the domain. Constrained EM: E step by expected dwell times and jump counts (Holmes and Rubin 2002, doi:10.1006/jmbi.2002.5405 [recalled]), M step strictly concave in flux coordinates with a 20-variable convex dual (normalization inside, branch lengths fixed) or a 19-variable dual on the cone followed by a compensating branch rescale (Theorem 4); the two conditional steps are different and produce different iterates (check 9: on an $n=4$ instance the normalized step beats the rescaled cone step by $0.18$ at fixed exposures [instance], and the compensated rescale preserves transition matrices to $5.6\times10^{-17}$). IQ-TREE has no expected-substitution-count machinery for substitution models (its EM is over mixture weights), so EM is an external reference only. A quadratic penalty on $\|\pi^*Q\|$ is not exact at finite weight and is admissible only as an initializer; metric projection of an unconstrained fit onto the fiber is a transport with a declared metric, not the constrained MLE.

## 4. nQMaker inside IQ-TREE 3 at commit 63c330d9, traced

### 4.1 The two outer loops

`-S` or `-Q` (`BRLEN_OPTIMIZE` or `TOPO_UNLINKED`): `PartitionModel::optimizeParameters` (`model/partitionmodel.cpp:883-958`). Each of up to `model_opt_steps = 10` rounds runs, in parallel over partitions, `ModelFactory::optimizeParameters` for that partition (branch lengths, then model parameters, which are fixed for a linked model, then site-rate parameters, with tolerances divided by $\min(K,10)$), then `optimizeLinkedModels`, and stops when the gain is below $10\,\epsilon_{\log L}$. This is the paper's 3b followed by 3a. `-p` (`BRLEN_SCALE`): `PartitionModelPlen::optimizeParameters` (`model/partitionmodelplen.cpp:75-194`), up to `num_param_iterations` rounds of per-partition rate parameters, linked model, partition rate multipliers, then branch lengths with $\min(5,i+1)$ sweeps. In both loops the linked-matrix step is `PartitionModel::optimizeLinkedModel` (`partitionmodel.cpp:753-840`).

### 4.2 The linked-matrix step

`optimizeLinkedModels` (`partitionmodel.cpp:842-868`) iterates `linked_models` (a map from model name to the first partition's model, the representative), un-fixes every partition's model with that name, calls `optimizeLinkedModel`, re-fixes, checkpoints and dumps. `optimizeLinkedModel`: `ndim = model->getNDim()`; `setVariables` packs from the representative; `((ModelMarkov*)model)->setBounds(...)`; `minimizeMultiDimen(variables, ndim, lower, upper, bound_check, max(gradient_epsilon, TOL_RATE))` runs on the `PartitionModel` object itself; `getVariables` broadcasts the result to every partition whose model name matches (line 737-744); if anything changed every such model is re-decomposed and the supertree likelihood recomputed. `minimizeMultiDimen` (`utils/optimization.cpp:750-778`) wraps `dfpmin` (BFGS on the inverse Hessian, `ITMAX = 200`, `EPS = 3.0e-8`, `TOLX = 4 EPS`, `STPMX = 100`, lines 781-900), with random restarts only for coordinates flagged in `bound_check`, which `ModelMarkov::setBounds` sets false (line 1148), so `NONREV` never restarts. Bounds are `[MIN_RATE, MAX_RATE] = [1e-4, 100]` (`model/modelmarkov.h:30, 32`), enforced by clamping the trial point in `fixBound` (`optimization.cpp:149-156`, called from `lnsrch` at line 684). Gradients are one-sided forward differences with step $10^{-4}|x_i|$ (section 3.2.5). The probe point $x_i+h_i$ is not clamped [source].

### 4.3 What happens to frequencies before the loop

The `PartitionModel` constructor (`partitionmodel.cpp:35-169`): for each linked model whose `freq_type` is `FREQ_ESTIMATE` or `FREQ_EMPIRICAL`, it pools state counts over all partitions with that model name (`countStates`, with unknown states added for missing sequences unless `TOPO_UNLINKED`), converts them by `convertCountToFreq` (8-round ambiguity fixed point, then `convfreq` floor), prints them as "Mean state frequencies", and calls `adaptStateFrequency` on every partition's model (lines 116-168) [source]. For `NONREV+FO` this rescales the LG-derived seed columns by $\pi^{\rm pooled}_j/\pi^{LG}_j$ (Theorem 3), a valid swap for the reversible seed, and `state_freq` is then overwritten on the first `decomposeRateMatrixNonrev` because `FREQ_ESTIMATE` keeps both guards true (lines 1252, 1266). The block is skipped for `FREQ_USER_DEFINED` (`continue` at line 118-119). Consequence for the constrained class: declare `FREQ_USER_DEFINED`, and the class receives the seed unmodified and $\pi^*$ intact.

### 4.4 `NONREV+F{...}` is a nonstationary-root model

With `FREQ_USER_DEFINED` and `optimize_from_given_params` false, both guards in `decomposeRateMatrixNonrev` are false: `state_freq` is neither reset nor re-solved, so the root distribution is the user vector while $Q$ is fitted without any constraint tying the two (lines 1252-1267) [source]. This is a legitimate model (a nonstationary root), not the constrained model. The `readParameters` consistency check (lines 1834-1843, and the string variant 1869-1878) warns when a supplied $\pi$ differs from the solved one by more than $10^{-3}$; it is the only place where a supplied $\pi$ is compared to $Q$ [source]. The paper's remark that `+F` is not valid for nonreversible models [paper, p. 1113] refers to `+F` without a vector, which `decomposeRateMatrixNonrev` overwrites.

### 4.5 Where `setRates` sits

`ModelMarkov::setRates()` is `ASSERT(0 && ...)` (`modelmarkov.cpp:1949-1952`) and is not called by the base `getVariables` (lines 1038-1089), which copies `variables` into `rates[]` directly [source]. `ModelUnrest` overrides `setRates` to pin the last rate (`modelunrest.cpp:106-111`) but its `getVariables` is the base one, so `setRates` runs only at construction; its `setStateFrequency` is a no-op (lines 113-115); it saves and restores the full `rates[]` array and calls `decomposeRateMatrix()` and `clearAllPartialLH()` after restore (lines 117-138) [source]. `ModelLieMarkov` is the one nonreversible class that maps a separate `model_parameters` vector to `rates[]` inside `getVariables` (`modelliemarkov.cpp:919-931`) [source], the closest precedent to a chart.

### 4.6 The derivative rule cannot be changed from the model class in linked training

`PartitionModel` derives from `ModelFactory`, which derives from `Optimization` (`model/modelfactory.h:64`); `PartitionModel` overrides `getNDim` and `targetFunk` but not `derivativeFunk` (`model/partitionmodel.h:118, 126`). `optimizeLinkedModel` calls `minimizeMultiDimen` on `this`, so `dfpmin` calls `Optimization::derivativeFunk` on the `PartitionModel`, which calls `PartitionModel::targetFunk`. A `derivativeFunk` override in a model subclass therefore affects single-alignment `ModelMarkov::optimizeParameters` (`modelmarkov.cpp:1199`) and not nQMaker's linked path. Any change to the finite-difference rule for linked training must be made in `Optimization::derivativeFunk` or in a new `PartitionModel::derivativeFunk`. This is the concrete reason positive coordinates (C2) are the low-deviation choice.

### 4.7 Other facts used below

`getNDim` returns `num_params` for a nonreversible model unless `fixed_parameters` (lines 964-975); `getNDimFreq` returns 0 except for `FREQ_EMPIRICAL` and codon types (978-1000); `PartitionModel::getNParameters` counts each linked model once by temporarily un-fixing it (242-256); `ModelFactory::getNParameters` adds `getNDim() + getNDimFreq()` plus site-rate and branch parameters [source]. `targetFunk` returns $10^{30}$ when any `state_freq` entry is in $(0,10^{-4})$, so $\pi^*$ must have all entries at least `min_state_freq`; IQ-TREE's own estimator floors at exactly that value (`alignment.cpp:5898-5901`) [source]. `reportModel` (`main/phyloanalysis.cpp:581-660`) prints the full $Q$ from `getQMatrix` (the normalized `rate_matrix`) and `state_freq` for a protein model with `getNDim() > 20` [source]. ModelFinder refuses a candidate set that mixes reversible and nonreversible names and requires `--nonrev-model` for nonreversible names (`main/phylotesting.cpp:448-486, 1158-1159`; `utils/tools.cpp:2910-2912, 2922-2924`) [source]; the check recognizes only the six built-in `NQ.*` names, so how a custom nonreversible matrix file behaves in `-mset` was not traced [unverified]. The `--eigen` path is broken for nonreversible models in release builds and `--init-model DIVMAT` is disabled by an `ASSERT` that vanishes under `NDEBUG` (as `AA_MODEL_INFERENCE.md` §15 states; the `ASSERT(0 && "init_by_div_mat not working")` at `partitionmodel.cpp:96` was read) [source]; neither is used here.

## 6. Specification of the constrained class (C2)

Provisional name `ModelNonRevFixedFreq`, model string `NQC` (the IQ-TREE maintainers choose the final syntax; a `NONREV` frequency suffix would collide with the live `NONREV+F{...}` semantics of 4.4 and is not recommended). Derives from `ModelMarkov` (or `ModelProtein`) constructed nonreversible; follows `ModelUnrest` for checkpointing and `ModelLieMarkov` for the parameter-to-rates mapping.

Construction. Accept `+F{p1,...,p20}` (required; `+FO`, `+F`, `+FQ` rejected with a message naming `NONREV` as the model for a free or solved equilibrium). Validate: 20 entries, each $\ge$ `min_state_freq`, renormalize to sum one, store as `state_freq` and as an immutable copy `target_freq`. Set `freq_type = FREQ_USER_DEFINED` and `ignore_state_freq = true` as for nonreversible models. Seed: read the exchangeabilities of LG (default) or of `--init-model NAME|FILE`; for a reversible seed, write $\pi^*$ into `state_freq` first and only then call `setReversible(false)`, so that the converted seed is $R_{ij}\pi^*_j$ (seed A of 3.1.5) rather than $R_{ij}\pi^{\rm seed}_j$; a nonreversible seed is taken as is. Then for each row $i$ choose `ref[i] = argmax_j rates[i][j]`, divide the row by `rates[i][ref[i]]` and multiply by `AA_SCALE`. `num_params = 360`. Build a fixed index map from the 360 variable slots to the 380 `rates[]` positions that skips `ref[i]` in each row.

`getNDim`: `fixed_parameters ? 0 : 360`. `getNDimFreq`: 0.

`setVariables(x)`: copy `rates[k]` for the 360 non-pinned positions into `x[1..360]` through the index map. `getVariables(x)`: compare and copy back through the index map; return whether anything changed (the pinned entries never change). No call to `setRates` is needed because the build lives in `decomposeRateMatrixNonrev`; if the maintainers prefer the `ModelLieMarkov` pattern, put the row-normalization in `setRates` and call it from `getVariables`.

`decomposeRateMatrixNonrev` (override): (1) unpack `rates[]` into the off-diagonals of a working matrix $W$ as the base does; (2) $p_{ij}=W_{ij}/\sum_{k\ne i}W_{ik}$; (3) form $P-I$ and call `computeStateFreqFromQMatrix(P - I, nu, n)` (it solves $[\mathbf 1^\top;\,(P-I)^\top]\nu=e_1$ by column-pivoted Householder QR, `modelmarkov.cpp:2119-2131`, and asserts $|\sum\nu-1|<10^{-4}$); (4) $\lambda_i=\nu_i/\pi^*_i$, `rate_matrix[i][j] = lambda_i p_ij`, `rate_matrix[i][i] = -lambda_i`; (5) do not touch `state_freq`; (6) compute the mean rate and rescale as the base does (it is 1 up to round-off; keep the step so the normalization is explicit); (7) in every build type, compute $\max_j|(\pi^*Q)_j|$ and $|m-1|$ and fail loudly (not `ASSERT`, which vanishes under `NDEBUG`) if either exceeds $10^{-8}$; (8) continue to the complex eigendecomposition exactly as the base (lines 1292-1387), including the scaling-and-squaring fallback that `computeTransMatrixNonrev` takes for a defective or ill-conditioned matrix (lines 463-505). Do not derive from the base's guard on `optimize_from_given_params`; this class never solves for $\pi$.

`setBounds`: lower `MIN_RATE`, upper `MAX_RATE` on all 360 coordinates, `bound_check = false`, as `ModelUnrest` (lines 85-93). A class-specific widening (for instance upper $10^4$) is a declared numerical choice, to be reported with bound hits.

`setStateFrequency` and `adaptStateFrequency`: no-ops that keep `target_freq` (the constructor path of 4.3 is already skipped by `FREQ_USER_DEFINED`; the no-op guards against other callers).

Checkpoint: save and restore the full 380-entry `rates[]`, `ref[]`, and `target_freq`, then `decomposeRateMatrix()` and `clearAllPartialLH()` (`ModelUnrest` pattern, base called last on save and first on restore).

Reporting: `reportModel` prints $Q$ and `state_freq` unchanged; add the stationarity residual, the number of coordinates within $10^{-4}$ of a bound, and the reference columns to `writeInfo`. The exported matrix is readable back as a fixed nonreversible model, and its `readParameters` consistency check passes because the solved $\pi$ equals $\pi^*$.

Linked training: the class is a pure function of the variable vector plus constants (`target_freq`, `ref[]`) that every partition's instance receives identically because every partition parses the same `model_joint` string and the same seed; the broadcast in `PartitionModel::getVariables` works unchanged. ## 7. The target $\pi^*$

Compute $\pi^*$ once per training population from the uncleaned training alignments with a declared estimator. Using IQ-TREE's estimator on the concatenated uncleaned training alignments reproduces exactly what the `PartitionModel` constructor prints as "Mean state frequencies" for a linked `+FO` model: pooled counts over all sites and sequences, ambiguity codes (B, Z, J, X, gaps) distributed by the 8-round fixed point, entries below $10^{-4}$ raised to $10^{-4}$ with the deficit taken from the largest entry (`alignment.cpp:4886-4933, 5882-5909`) [source]. ## 8. Statistical caveats that the mathematics establishes

Composition misfit. By Theorem 5 the constrained model's tip marginals are $\pi^*$. On population pattern probabilities from a reversible 3-state process with composition $(0.6,0.3,0.1)$ on a three-tip rooted star, fitting at the correct target recovers the reversible generator (gain of the full model over the reversible model $<10^{-14}$ per site, cycle coordinate $10^{-6}$), while fitting at the wrong target $(0.5,0.3,0.2)$ gives the full model a gain of $4.6\times10^{-6}$ per site over the best reversible member and a gauge-fixed cycle coordinate of $-0.059$ (check 11 [instance]; this reproduces the audit's population example and supports Doc A's simulated T14 mechanism, whose specific magnitudes remain unreproduced). Circulation reported by the constrained fit is therefore confounded with the constraint. The reversible refit at $\pi^*$ cannot detect this, because it lacks the 171 directions. Required before any biological claim of nonreversibility: simulate under the composition-consistent reversible fit of the cleaned data (AliSim, fitted gene trees), fit the constrained class at $\pi^*$, and measure the circulation it reports against zero; then repeat with a nonreversible generating model and check recovery. A content-dependent site filter is the mechanism test; a fixed column mask is the outcome-independent control.

Nested tests. Section 2, Theorem 9. Fix rooted topology, root edge and root split identically under both hypotheses before quoting $\chi^2_{171}$; otherwise bootstrap the actual protocol. The 19-degree-of-freedom composition-cost statistic against `NONREV` describes the data and is expected to be large.

Non-concavity and starts. Theorem 8. Report the end points of at least two starts (D9); two charts or two starts agreeing is evidence about the optimizer, not a proof of global optimality.

Boundary policy. Interior charts with a box: a coordinate at $10^{-4}$ is a declared floor, not a zero rate; report bound hits; compare fits only on identical boxes.

No standard errors. `dfpmin` frees its inverse Hessian (`FREEALL`) without write-back [source]; even if copied out, after alternating nuisance optimization and tree selection it is not a defensible covariance. Use gene resampling.
