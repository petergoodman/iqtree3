## 1. Method

### 1.1 The chart, f = f0 + Zy

- Fluxes f_ij = π*_i q_ij on the n(n − 1) ordered pairs, in `t3._edges` order. Stationarity
  ((π*Q)_j = inflow − outflow = 0) and unit mean rate (Σf = 1) are Cf = b with C = [D; 1ᵀ] (n − 1
  balance rows and the normalization row), b = e_n. rank C = n: the ones vector lies in ker D, so it is
  orthogonal to D's rows. dim ker C = n(n − 2): 3, 8, 360 at n = 3, 4, 20 **(derived; run: SVD at n = 3,
  4, 20)**. The polytope does not depend on π*, which enters only through q_ij = f_ij / π*_i.
- Canonical form, for the map tests: f0 = 1/(n(n − 1)) uniform, balanced and inside every ratio box
  **(run: ‖Cf0 − b‖ ≤ 1.1e-15)**; Z = `scipy.linalg.null_space(C)`, orthonormal, Zᵀ1 = 0 **(run)**;
  y = Zᵀ(f − f0). Being linear, it is accurate in absolute, not relative, terms: a round trip at fluxes
  down to 7.8e-14 had relative errors to 4.0e-5 (review's run).
- **Solver form (proposed): a re-centred scaled basis.** At a centre f_c in P_box, f = f_c ⊙ (1 + N_c u)
  with N_c = `null_space(C · diag(f_c))`. diag(f_c) N_c spans ker C **(run by the review: residual
  4.8e-17, dimension 360)**, so this is still f = f0 + Zy with linear constraints; u measures relative
  flux changes, like log coordinates near f_c. Why: the likelihood's curvature in a flux scales roughly
  as 1/f (expected counts proportional to f), and fluxes at case A's peak span about 5.6e11, so an
  orthonormal flux basis leaves the solver a Hessian conditioned near 1e11 to 1e12 (derived from the
  measured range). The fit restarts with f_c at its endpoint until a restart gains less than 1e-4, at
  most 10 restarts. No projection at a restart (it would move a 1e-13 flux by about 1e-5 relative,
  review); the centre is checked to lie strictly inside P_box, and A3's residuals cover drift.
- **Build:** a non-finite or non-positive flux is refused before any likelihood is computed; Q = f / π*
  off the diagonal, diagonal from row sums. No renormalization: Σf = 1 holds by construction and the
  residuals are checked, not repaired.
- **Independence:** the fitting path uses only `t3._edges`, `cases.generator_from_flux`, the likelihood
  and SciPy. References and `chart.inverse` enter only through the matched domain and the common
  diagnostics.

### 1.2 Domains (D03)

- **Matched domain: NQC's box, exactly.** z_ij = log(K_ij / K_i,r(i)) = log(f_ij / f_i,r(i)), since
  K_ij = q_ij / λ_i and π*_i cancels within a row **(derived)**. So lo ≤ z_ij ≤ hi is
  e^lo f_i,r(i) ≤ f_ij ≤ e^hi f_i,r(i): 2 n(n − 2) homogeneous linear inequalities (720 at n = 20).
  With Cf = b they imply strict positivity **(derived; proof corrected by the review)**: if f_i,r(i) = 0
  the upper constraints zero row i; zero outflow from i means zero inflow by balance, so f_ki = 0 for
  every k; then each row k is zero, by its lower constraint at destination i (or because i = r(k));
  so f = 0, against Σf = 1. Hence P_box = {Cf = b, ratio constraints} is a compact convex polytope equal
  to the image of NQC's box under the chart's bijection, for the same references and [lo, hi] **(run:
  200 box points, 20 at corners, satisfy the inequalities to a relative slack of −4.4e-16 of the bound;
  z round trip 2.7e-15; a hit-and-run point of P_box maps into the box)**. D03's warning that boxes in
  different charts are different sets does not apply: one set, two charts. It changes with the
  references.
- **Open domain (reported only):** f > 0, the full positive family, kept interior by the barrier; final
  barrier parameter and smallest flux recorded. No flux floor: even 1e-12 would cut off part of P_box,
  whose fluxes reach 1.2e-13 at case A's peak **(run)**.

### 1.3 Objective and gradient

- F(u) = −lnL(Q(u)) through `objective.log_likelihood(Q)`; `LinkedObjective` gains `log_likelihood(Q)`,
  the sum over parts in order.
- **Gradient: analytic, in the oracle only (Peter, Q1 and R1).** A new `likelihood.log_likelihood_gradient`
  returns lnL and G = ∂lnL/∂Q from one upward and one downward pass per rate category and, per branch c
  and category k, the adjoint of the matrix exponential's Fréchet derivative:
  G = Σ_c Σ_k r_k t_c L(r_k t_c Qᵀ, W_ck), W_ck the count-weighted outer products of outside and inside
  partials over L_s, scaling handled in log space. The adjoint identity was checked to 0 relative
  difference on a 20-state case **(run)**; the formula and chain rule were checked by the review
  **(read by the review)**. Chain rule: ∂lnL/∂f_ij = (G_ij − G_ii)/π*_i, then
  ∂F/∂u = −(diag(f_c) N_c)ᵀ ∂lnL/∂f. The z-gradient for the reference climber is
  −⟨G, ∂Q/∂z_k⟩ with `chart.derivative` per coordinate. Refused: `+I` (no S5 problem uses it) and a
  nonzero root-branch length (run02's tree ends `):0.0000000000;` and `toy.tree_text` writes none
  **(read)**).
  - Against finite differences: in the scaled basis they would be accurate, but each gradient costs
    361 to 721 evaluations (about 4 s on T3 on 11 workers, review), BFGS asks for one at every trial
    point **(read: `ScalarFunction._update_x`)**, and their rounding noise (about 1e-12 |lnL| / h) keeps
    a projected gradient from going much below 1e-4 to 1e-5, which A5 needs (section 4).

### 1.4 Solver, its stopping setting, and validation before any comparison

- **SciPy 1.17 `minimize(method="trust-constr")` (Peter, Q2)**, interior point, one
  `LinearConstraint(G_u, h_u, ∞, keep_feasible=True)` in u, the analytic gradient; no new dependency
  (decision 013). **(read)** `keep_feasible` reaches the solver as `enforce_feasibility`: the slack is
  set to −c(x) and a non-positive slack gives the barrier −∞ log, so an infeasible trial is rejected;
  the objective is still evaluated there first, so the objective returns +∞ for an infeasible flux.
  With the BFGS update the gradient is also requested at every trial, so an infeasible trial would
  corrupt the update: the gradient raises, the fit stops and is recorded as failed (the review saw no
  infeasible trial in its runs; linear constraints and the 0.995 fraction-to-boundary make them a
  rounding event). A start must be strictly feasible (a start on the boundary stalled at μ = 0.1 for
  5,000 iterations, review's run): every start has its coordinates within 1e-9 of a bound moved 1e-9
  inside in z before mapping, and the lnL change is recorded.
- **Declared stopping setting (decision 039; review blocker 1).** **(read)** The interior point stops
  with status 1 when optimality and constraint violation are below `gtol`, with no condition on the
  barrier parameter μ; at SciPy's defaults the review measured stops at μ = 1.3e-6 to 6.4e-6, where 165
  active constraints cost up to 1e-3 in lnL. So the fit is stopped by a callback (status 3) when
  μ ≤ 1e-10 and the analytic projected z-gradient of its endpoint is at most 1e-6 (A5), with
  `gtol` = 1e-14 so status 1 cannot pre-empt it, `xtol` = 1e-14, `barrier_tol` = 1e-12,
  `initial_barrier_parameter` 0.1 (default), and caps of 50,000 iterations and 4 h wall time per fit.
  A fit reaching a cap or any other status is unconverged and counted. m·μ (m active constraints) is
  recorded for every fit.
- **Two declared Hessian configurations, chosen by validation only:** (1) SciPy's `BFGS` update in the
  re-centred scaled basis; (2) Newton: the Hessian by forward differences of the analytic gradient in
  the scaled basis, probes kept feasible, spread over a process pool. Configuration 1 is used if it
  passes every validation check (V1, V1b, V2, V3); otherwise configuration 2. If configuration 1 later
  fails A5 on a comparison problem, configuration 2 is run on every problem; configuration 2's results
  then decide the gate and configuration 1's are reported. The choice never looks at agreement with
  NQC. Every configuration and setting tried is logged in the commit message and the CHANGELOG entry.
- **Reference climber in z:** `lbfgsb_fit` gains an optional analytic-gradient pass (default behaviour
  unchanged; decision 017's port is untouched), stopped at an analytic projected gradient of 1e-6, with
  `maxcor` 20 and the same caps; its final central-difference gradient is kept for A5(ii).
- **Endpoint certificate:** the reference's KKT residual in u, with multipliers of the active
  constraints from bounded least squares, is recorded; for the concave validation problems it gives a
  rigorous optimality-gap bound (section 4, V1).
- **Not chosen** (Q2): SLSQP (dense BFGS, no scaling control; used by the design script only at n = 3
  **(read)**); a custom log-barrier Newton method (new optimizer code in a reference; the fallback if
  both SciPy configurations fail validation, then Peter decides); cyipopt (a new pinned dependency).

## 2. Equivalence with NQC

| Element | NQC | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Data | the run's alignment | the same files through the oracle's readers |
| Nuisances | Level 1: `-te` input tree text, `-blfix`, braced rates (decision 024) | the same tree text (never `.treefile`), α or `-S` trees, fixed |
| Target | `NQC+F{...}` parsed by the class | `target.parse_target`; bitwise against the checkpoint's `target` |
| References | row maxima of LG at π* (decision 011) | `nqc.lg_seed`; bitwise against the checkpoint's `references` |
| Domain | `--nqc-domain 1e-5,1e5` passed explicitly, as `convergence.py` names its domain | P_box for the same references and bounds |
| Objective | IQ-TREE's −lnL | the oracle's, equal within 1e-8 × \|lnL\| at a fixed Q (021) |
| Start | LG at π* | the same Q mapped to flux |

Only Level 1 is compared: the reference fits Q alone, as step 3a does. Level 2, `-q` and the
GTR20-seeded start are covered by S2 to S3b and not repeated. No problem has identical sequences
(`twopart.problems` at creation), so IQ-TREE's removal of them does not arise.

Where the two may legitimately differ:
1. **Active faces.** NQC's line search and `lbfgsb_fit` clamp exactly onto a bound; the interior point
   stops a distance of about μ/|g| inside, costing about μ per active constraint. With μ ≤ 1e-10 that is
   below 1e-7 for 720 constraints.
2. **Weakly identified rates.** Unobserved substitutions leave −lnL nearly flat along some directions
   (S3c: 164 coordinates at the lower bound, 0.022 to gain beyond the box **(read)**), and converged
   climbs from different starts at `pgtol` 1e-3 ended 0.303, 0.017 and 9.7e-4 apart in S3c's cases A to
   C (P2 against P2w **(read)**). Hence the tight stopping test (A5) and the line rule of A6. Matrices are
   reported, not asserted, with a segment profile: P_box is convex, so the segment between two endpoints
   in flux space is feasible, and lnL along it tells a ridge from a valley.
3. **Distinct local maxima** (D09 item 7): the outcome table of section 4.
4. **NQC stops short** at default and relaxed settings (decision 039; S3c 0.77 to 4.2 and 0.67 to 2.5
   **(read)**).
5. **Open domain:** its peak may lie beyond the box; the difference is the box's cost, reported.
6. **Rejections:** NQC refuses softmax underflow and non-positive ν, neither possible inside the box; the
   reference refuses non-positive flux.

**Outcome table (pre-declared).** Status of each fit: converged (A5), unconverged (cap, other status, A5

2. All converged: A6, A7, A8 evaluated.
3. A6 or A7 fails: cross-starts between the disagreeing pair H (higher) and W (lower), each moved 1e-9
   inside:
   - H's fitter from W reaches within L of H, and W's fitter from H stays within L of H: **W stopped
     short**, a convergence failure of W's fitter;
   - a cross-start from H gains more than L: **H not at a peak**, a failure of H's fitter;
   - both cross-starts stay within L of their starts: **distinct stationary points**; the segment
     profile labels them **one ridge** (lnL along the segment never below lnL(W) − L) or **distinct
     peaks**;
   - a cross-start ends more than L below its start, or fails A5: **cross-start unconverged**;
   - anything else: **undetermined**.
   The failed check stays failed in every case; the label is the diagnosis given to Peter.
4. A8 fails: reported with F_e (the reference from NQC's endpoint) as evidence of where the climbs
   part.
